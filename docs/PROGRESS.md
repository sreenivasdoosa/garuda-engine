# Garuda Engine — progress tracker

**Updated:** 2026-10-10

The living record of what has landed. `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` says what we
intend to build and in what order; this says what exists today, what is still
vocabulary, and what comes next.

**Keep this current.** Update it whenever a chunk of work lands — a commit or
a run of related commits — in the same change, not afterwards. A tracker
brought up to date once a week is a tracker nobody trusts. Two rules keep it
honest:

- **Say what is not built, not only what is.** A feature is not delivered
  because a table, an enum member or a config column exists for it. The
  section on vocabulary below is the most useful part of this file.
- **One fact, one place.** Where a design document already explains something,
  link to it rather than restating it.

## Where we are

**The engine is through Phase 3 and well into Phase 4. The Console is
served for the market group, most of the accounts group, the Terminal,
the Strategy Engine and Strategy Config, the risk group, the Trade Log,
System Status, Audit Logs, Broker Instruments, Reports, Analytics and Alerts.** Thirty pages work
against the real backend — auth, config, health, exchanges, holidays, special
trading days, event days, symbols, stock universes, data providers, market
data, the Live Feed, trading clients, brokers, broker exchange configs,
Broker Instruments, the Terminal, strategy definitions, rules and
subscriptions, strategy configuration, RMS Config, RMS Breaches, Kill
Switches, RMS Daily Stats, the Trade Log and its timeline, System Status,
Audit Logs, Reports, Analytics, and Alerts. System Config and Corporate
Actions still call endpoints nothing serves, so
by the plan's own rule —
*a phase is not done until its Console pages work against the real backend*
— no phase past 0 is closed.

Everything below the API line runs. `garuda seed`, `garuda check` and
`garuda serve` build the whole engine, load strategies, evaluate rules, size
and place orders, manage open positions and square off, and serve the Console
and its socket from the same process.

## Built and running

**Foundations** — layer contracts enforced by import-linter; `Decimal`-only
money paths and the clock discipline both lint-enforced; mypy strict; the
domain model and its property tests.

**Persistence and recovery** — the schema and its migrations, the seed loader,
the append-only journal in the same transaction as the row it describes,
startup reconciliation that halts rather than auto-corrects, and the trade
store that survives a restart.

**Market data** — the Zerodha instrument master and tick feed; the tick hub;
the instrument registry with expiry and strike selection; candle history
fetched from the broker and cached in memory; five synthetic instruments
(rolling straddle, PCR, synthetic future, implied volatility, IV skew);
Black-Scholes and an implied-volatility solver.

**Strategy engine** — strategies composed from specs and configuration rows
rather than subclasses; four-scope config resolution merged per field; a rule
engine with three-valued outcomes (pass, fail, **unavailable**); 12 rules,
7 direction rules, 6 selectors, 11 indicators; tranche lifecycle; strike
selection by moneyness and by premium; an adapter that reads the Console's own
`operator`/`condition` rule JSON.

**Order and trade management** — the order state machine with client order ids
and idempotency; entry, protective and square-off services; freeze-quantity
slicing; the per-tick pass over open positions; per-leg trailing (risk
multiple, ATR, EMA, SuperTrend) and trail-to-cost; combined stop, target and
trail across a group, with the group's high-water mark persisted on its legs.

**Risk** — the gate in front of every order, entries and exits alike, with
each check declaring whether it may stop an exit; limits resolved per order
across all four `rms_config` scopes; 15 checks; scoped kill switches; order
rate limits; every refusal recorded in `rms_breach_log`.

**The trade log** — every event in a trade's life written as it happens, at
the reference's own call sites: entry placed, filled, rejected or failed;
the stop placed, rejected and trailed; the target placed, or the exit order
once the trade is leaving; the exit triggered, failed or given up on; the
trade completed. One row per event in `trade_log`, read back as the
cross-trade log and as one trade's timeline.

**System Status** — the engine read end to end for one page: the feed with
the reference's one-word verdict (up, down while a market is open, inactive
otherwise), the runner and the trade and strategy loops, every account and
why it is not trading, the brokers, each venue's day and next phase, the
risk books, the instrument files, the process's own memory and file
handles, a quote-and-history probe on demand, and the two init timelines
-- what starting up took, step by step, and what each phase's task took
today -- ported from the reference's in-memory init recorder.

**The audit log** — every operator mutation, recorded once it succeeded,
with who and what it was before. Captured where nothing can bypass it:
the session's flush sees each row the API inserts, edits in place or
deletes, with the old values from the ORM's own history; the repository
records the statements that never flush an object. One middleware turns
the capture into `audit_log` rows after a successful response, in the
reference's vocabulary -- a lone flag flip is ENABLE or DISABLE, a session
row is LOGIN or LOGOUT -- plus the buttons that change the engine without
a row: start, stop, restart, square-off, download. Secrets are redacted
before they are written. Read as the reference's page: filtered, searched
and paged in SQL, one entity's history, one user's.

## Named but not built

The most useful section here. Each of these has a table, an enum member or a
configuration column, and nothing behind it. They are named so a ported
configuration is refused loudly rather than silently ignored.

| | |
|---|---|
| **14 of 29 breach types** | `PRICE_FREAK`, `VOLATILITY_CIRCUIT`, `MARGIN_INSUFFICIENT`, `DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT`, `POSITION_TOTAL_EXCEEDED`, `POSITION_PER_STRATEGY_EXCEEDED`, `COMBO_TOTAL_EXCEEDED`, `BROKER_STOPPED`, `ERROR_RATE_CIRCUIT`, `PRICE_DEVIATION`, `PRICE_NOT_TRADED_TODAY`, `DUPLICATE_EXIT_ORDER`, `EXIT_RATE_EXCEEDED`, `ORDER_OPERATION_RATE_EXCEEDED` |
| **4 exit reasons** | `MAX_HOLDING`, `SIGNAL_FLIP`, `PORTFOLIO_STOP_LOSS`, decay |
| **1 trailing mode** | `HEIKIN_ASHI` — needs a candle transform, a wick search and a distance cap that nothing else uses |
| **Automatic kill switches** | Operator-set switches load and fire, and the Kill Switches page sets, turns off and removes them while the engine runs. The reference also raises them from a daily loss, a volatility circuit or a rejection rate, with a state machine for when one may re-fire. `DailyLossCheck` refuses at the limit instead. The per-source arm/disarm flags are served and honoured by the loader, and the page says nothing raises a switch on its own yet. |
| **The trade log's retention and its hedge events** | The reference purges a finished trade's timeline after sixty days, nightly; garuda has no nightly sweep yet (the finished-trade archive is not scheduled either), so the table grows until one lands. Of the forty event types, the hedge-replace, order-modified, escalation, `SIGNAL_GENERATED` and `PARAMETERS_CHANGED` events are named and never written, because the paths that would write them -- hedge replacement, exit repricing, the escalation ladder -- are not built. |
| **Eleven of forty-one risk columns are enforced** | The gate reads the order size and value caps, the daily loss amount, the position cap per symbol, the four order rate windows, the stale-price window, the spread and the volume floor. The rest -- depth, freak price, price deviation, early-market grace, open interest, lots per order, freeze-quantity check, total positions and combos, buy/sell splits, daily loss percentage, auto square-off and auto-kill, rejection rate, VIX -- are stored, served and marked as not enforced on the page. |
| **The `*_policy` tables** | Not read, and that is correct: they are Console-side templates with no key from a strategy. See `TRADE_MANAGEMENT.md`. |
| **The phase record is in memory** | The runner's journal-backed phase recorder exists (`journal/phases.py`) and is wired nowhere: `runtime.start` hands the runner an in-memory one, so a restart forgets which phases ran and replays yesterday's and today's -- the System Status page's day timeline now shows this happening at every start. Harmless while the tasks are idempotent loads and starts; it stops being harmless the day a phase squares off or archives. |
| **System Status: what it cannot see** | The process figures (resident memory, system memory, open files, load) are read from `/proc` and are null elsewhere. The probe's tick half reads the hub's last price for `NSE:NIFTY_50` rather than pulling a quote over REST, because nothing in the engine pulls a quote yet (see "Quotes have no caller"); its history half is the network round-trip the reference makes. The reference's trade-processor, thread-pool, servlet, licence, eviction, archive and queue panels are not ported: they describe a multi-tenant JVM. |
| **Most of the API** | Serving now: config, login, profile, change-password, health, exchanges, holidays, special trading days, event days, symbols, stock universes, data providers, market data, the ticker and instrument files, trading clients, brokers, broker exchange configs, broker API stats, Broker Instruments, the Terminal, the strategy pages, the risk pages (limits, breaches, kill switches, daily stats), the trade log, System Status, and Audit Logs. Every other page still calls endpoints nothing serves. `GET /api/exchanges/market-status` is the one exchange route still missing: it needs the built trading calendars, which live in `composition` — above `api` — so it lands with the Terminal, its only caller. |
| **Stock universes** | Both tables and `strategy_definitions.universe_id` exist and the endpoints serve them; nothing resolves a universe into subscriptions. That is engine work belonging with the strategy pages. |
| **`PREDEFINED_INDEX` universes** | Accepted, but garuda has no NSE CSV pipeline, so such a universe holds whatever was last written by hand and its `last_refreshed_at` never moves. `CUSTOM` is the usable type today. |
| **Market data from the four new providers** | Configuration, login and session storage work for Zerodha, Fyers, Dhan, XTS and TrueData. **Dhan is now complete as an adapter**: scrip master, catalogue, REST quotes, today's OHLC, candles and the binary websocket feed. What is *not* built: Fyers' HSM binary framing, XTS's Socket.IO, TrueData's websocket, and the masters, quotes and history for those three. |
| **A trading client's login does not refresh the instrument files** | A *data provider's* login does, through `renew_after_login`. The Trading Clients page has no API yet, so the broker-side login that should do the same has nowhere to hook in; it lands with that page. |
| **Quotes have no caller** | `QuoteSource` is a protocol, Dhan implements it, and `quote_factory` now reaches it — but nothing in the engine pulls a quote yet. The pipeline still prices from the last tick. The fallback-when-the-feed-is-stalled path the protocol describes is not built. |
| **Users, in the Console** | The pages that scope by *user* -- the user selector, event days, recompute, subscriptions, the config tree -- still speak of users. In garuda that concept is the trading client, whose wire shape `/api/trading-clients` now defines; the rename of those pages is still to do. |
| **An account that logs out, or whose broker is stopped, keeps trading until restart** | Admission is one-way. A session that expires or is logged out fails every order with an auth error and an alert, which stops entries; a broker stopped on the Brokers page while running stops nothing until restart. The kill switch is the runtime brake for both. Retiring an account's parts while it may hold positions is a decision not yet made. |
| **Segment hours take effect at restart** | Like the venue's own hours and holidays, a segment's hours are read when the engine builds its venues. The Exchanges page says so on save. A runtime reload of the venues is one change for all of them. |
| **One instrument master** | Broker Instruments lists only the brokers the engine builds a master for, which is Zerodha's; the page names the others as not built rather than offering a download that cannot happen. |
| **Three columns on a broker exchange config are read by nothing** | The login and the two square-off minute columns. Garuda takes the venue's day from the Exchanges page; they stay on the row because the reference's did and the page shows them. |

## Deliberate departures from the plan

Recorded in `SCOPE_DECISIONS.md`; listed here so the two do not drift.

- **The strategy engine is being rebuilt to the reference's shape** without
  its templates (`STRATEGY_ENGINE.md`, decided 2026-10-15). Until that lands,
  what runs is the withdrawn rules design: a one-second sweep over JSON entry
  rules, no scheduled tranches, strikes from leg selectors, lots sized per leg
  against the whole capital. Do not build on it.
- **External signals are deferred**, not out: the event type will exist from
  the start, the producer later (`TODO.md`).
- **Candle history is cached in memory, not Postgres.** Settled days fetched
  once, today refreshed when stale.
- **11 indicators, not 16.** The absent five are ones no configured strategy
  in the reference uses, and adding one costs no rule.
